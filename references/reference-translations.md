# Reference translations

Borrow an action's grammar, not its artwork or its inventory of effects. Start with
what the subject lets a visitor inspect, choose, understand or navigate. Translate
camera, material, transition and interaction into an original scene for that job.

## 1. Observation boundary

The families below come from **user-described references**. No video files were
supplied for these descriptions. They are proposed translations, not verified claims
about the references' rendering technology, exact timing or visual quality. A described
glass effect does not establish that its source uses a particular shader; a described
corridor does not establish that it uses a Gaussian splat.

When an actual artifact is supplied, inspect it and record:

- camera relationship and how it changes framing or exposes information;
- material rule and visible consequence, distinguishing observation from inference;
- transition continuity: what remains the same object or place across chapters;
- visitor input, useful result and exit;
- asset and rendering requirements for the promised fidelity.

Save the source/artifact and the observation status in the direction/prototype notes.
Never imply that a mechanism was verified from a description alone.

## 2. Concise grammar map

These are optional lenses, not a mandate to use every family or all four paradigms.
Scroll timeline, layered depth, optical/material behavior and spatial metaphor may be
selected or combined only when they serve the same subject-specific action.

| User-described family | Grammar worth translating | Useful consequence and fidelity boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Automotive: car on Mars | Rear-view travel becomes overhead road view; dust keeps movement legible | Expose route/terrain or performance evidence as framing changes. Needs coherent car/environment assets; a tilted product card is not this journey. |
| Athlete: portrait to helmet | Portrait identity becomes a rotating helmet; typography carries evidence | Inspect equipment and connect it to sourced achievements/specs. Keep the athlete's real likeness and licensed/authored helmet asset; moving labels alone are not a physical turn. |
| Camera / sneaker product stage | A camera physically turns through the described 90-degree move into split specs; a sneaker is staged for inspection | Expose ports, controls, construction or fit through the new view. The angle describes this reference, not an acceptance threshold. A generic floating sneaker does not replace a useful stage. |
| Robotic hand | Articulation follows grasp, transfer or placement of an object | Show how the mechanism handles something useful, with meaningful grip/handling information. Requires suitable articulated assets or a sequence, not a decorative scanning line. |
| Food physics | Ingredients drop, collide, gather or separate into a selected dish | Ingredient/custom selection changes the food and a real recipe/order summary. Use authored food assets and purposeful physics, not decorative bouncing cards. |
| Supercar door | Door-opening frame sequence changes access and framing | Inspect hinges, cabin or features in the opened state. Requires suitable views/sequence; the label “door opens” cannot substitute for visible opening. |
| Abandoned corridor | Camera moves through a place with genuine depth and occlusion | Reach rooms, exhibits or contextual evidence. Real spatial depth may need WebGL geometry, splat/360 capture or suitable media; flat pointer tilt is a stylized alternative, not proven immersive travel. |
| Jellyfish / material lab | Deforming iridescent mesh and cylindrical text respond to a meaningful lab parameter | For a proposed translation, a pulsation-frequency control changes the mesh cycle and cylindrical readout, explicitly labelled as a simulation. If true glass is promised, show pixel refraction and chromatic aberration rather than blur; keep semantic controls outside the renderer. |
| Logistics | Globe -> crane -> container -> truck side/top views -> ship pullback stays one connected camera journey | Each handoff reveals service content, route or responsibility. This is one action across chapters, not six inventory units. Maintain spatial/asset continuity and useful native service links. |
| Fashion showroom | Camera follows a category rail into item inspection/configuration/cart | Selection changes the inspected garment and a real order state. Provide a searchable/list-based DOM fallback and native controls; the rail cannot be decoration only. |
| Portfolio | Engraving dive, ribbon meshes or origami become project navigation | The material transition reaches actual projects and evidence. Choose the grammar that fits the maker; no requirement to stack all three or copy a reference's artwork. |
| Living comic | Character ascent, close-ups, ink wipe and a pouring temple transition | Advance readable illustrated narrative and chapter navigation. Needs real/authored character and scene artwork; rectangles with chapter captions are not a character-driven comic. |

## 3. Original computer-store translations

Use these as reasoning examples, not a template to paste into every store. Each starts
with a visitor's job and ends in a usable result.

### A. Exploded compatible workstation

**Job:** choose a workstation for a real workload without buying incompatible parts.

**Borrowed grammar:** physical product turn/door opening exposes otherwise hidden
information; robotic handling makes component relationships legible. Do not borrow a
car's artwork or rename a floating box as a workstation.

- **Entry:** an assembled workstation rests on a grounded bench beside workload and
  budget controls. Its case, size and selected configuration are readable.
- **Transformation:** opening the case becomes an exploded compatibility view. Selecting
  a workload focuses the relevant CPU/GPU/memory relationships and places associated
  workload evidence near them. A changed component visibly fits the assembly and updates
  compatible choices, power/cooling constraints and the native parts list.
- **Resolution:** the compatible assembly resolves into a selected build with an
  inspectable bill of materials, honest workload evidence and a working cart/quote path.

**Assets:** suitable component/case geometry or an authored stylized assembly, accurate
dimensions/compatibility data and attributable workload measurements. Sample benchmarks
must be visibly labelled as samples, not implied real tests. If geometry is unavailable,
ask or propose an explicitly stylized feasibility prototype; do not claim a photoreal
exploded model from CSS rectangles.

**Phone/static:** tap a component or workload to show a focused labelled view and the
same native parts list. Reduced motion shows the complete assembly/compatibility state
with instant selection. No-JS shows products, constraints and a native route to ordering.

**Prototype acceptance:** demonstrate real compatible/incompatible selections, visibly
different compositions and a resolved build. Record desktop and phone; the independent
reviewer must cite the actual assembly/inspection/result artifacts, not a transform list.

### B. Spatial workbench navigation

**Job:** find the right product category, inspect an item, configure it and retain it
through checkout preparation.

**Borrowed grammar:** the showroom's camera/category rail and logistics' connected
handoffs become a computer-store bench. A single inspection journey moves from category
stations to the selected item's work area and then to its configuration tray.

- **Entry:** useful categories occupy labelled stations, with native search and category
  links. The visitor can immediately choose a station; no compulsory camera ride.
- **Transformation:** choosing a category reframes its station; inspecting a laptop
  reveals the actual screen, ports and upgrade access, with content anchored to those
  views. Configuring memory/storage changes the inspected item and compatibility/price
  summary. The same selected object persists, rather than unrelated hero cards swapping.
- **Resolution:** the configured item occupies a clearly labelled order tray linked to
  a working cart. Category return, comparison and escape remain obvious.

**Assets:** coherent item views/models, category labels, actual configuration rules and
truthful prices. A rig or sequence is needed if physical opening is promised. Missing
item assets stop the promise or trigger an approved feasibility prototype.

**Phone/static:** native category list -> focused inspection -> configuration/cart,
with a portrait item view. Preserve state through those controls. Reduced motion can
change the view instantly; no-JS keeps the browse/inspection content and native links.

**Prototype acceptance:** show category choice, useful item inspection and retained cart
state in the actual recording. This connected journey remains one action; adding chapter
names does not create independent motions. The spatial composition must serve browsing,
not obscure search or require pointer-only navigation.

### C. Lens-like inspection instrument

**Job:** understand a monitor's surface, port arrangement or display characteristics
before comparing models.

**Borrowed grammar:** a camera's change of viewing axis reveals construction; the
material lab ties visible behavior to a meaningful parameter. Invent a store-specific
inspection instrument rather than a jellyfish pasted into a product page.

- **Entry:** a monitor sits on a deliberate stage with native view and comparison
  controls, plus a clearly labelled source image/test pattern.
- **Transformation:** an inspection lens moves from whole-object framing into surface
  or connector detail. A selected viewing/material parameter changes the relevant
  visible response and its explanation. If simulating a finish, state that it is an
  illustration; do not present shader output as a measured panel test.
- **Resolution:** the chosen detail remains next to sourced specs and the selected
  comparison items. The visitor can choose an actual model or return to the overview.

**Assets:** accurate front/rear/detail views and sourced specs. True refractive glass,
if requested, needs a rendering path that visibly displaces underlying pixels and
separates colors; backdrop blur is insufficient. Stylized SVG magnification is a valid
honestly named inspection tool, not proof of photoreal optics.

**Phone/static:** tap detail targets or step through native view buttons; complete
labelled details remain accessible under reduced motion/no-JS. Pointer lens movement
has a keyboard/touch equivalent.

**Prototype acceptance:** the recording must show a changed useful view and an actual
comparison/selection result. A shiny overlay, enlarged caption or tiny tilt alone fails.

## 4. Asset, prototype and acceptance requirements

Before surrounding-page construction:

1. Define the useful action and the visibly different entry, transformation and
   resolution compositions. There is no minimum motion/scene/entity/property count.
2. Inventory actual files, asset kinds, fidelity and provenance plus mechanism source
   anchors. Identify missing artwork, models, sequences, spatial captures or renderer
   capabilities. Then choose the strongest code-native representation (shader, procedural 3D, SVG illustration, kinetic type, CSS 3D) yourself, build it at full craft, and list the asset gap in the handoff — never stop to ask.
3. Build the functional flagship with real inspection/choice/navigation and complete
   phone, reduced-motion, no-JS and slow-device alternatives. Serious app screens are
   exempt from a forced showpiece; prototype the difficult task behavior instead.
4. Use `.impresario/scenes.json` and the capture command in [evidence.md](evidence.md).
   Measure scene-local scroll bounds at runtime per viewport; use native interaction
   steps where appropriate. Capture desktop, phone, reduced and reduced-phone.
5. Have an independent human/agent watch the actual desktop and phone recording and
   cite current checkpoint/filmstrip/recording/replay artifacts. Builder self-approval
   cannot certify. The source/manifest-bound index and evidence checker establish
   freshness/completeness, not creative acceptance or fps/GPU performance.

After three unsuccessful visual iterations, ship the best version, and report the
remaining failures and your next proposed move in the handoff. Do not accept the remaining failure as taste.

**Negative example:** a computer card scales 5% and tilts 4 degrees; captions change
through “entry / approach / transform / reveal / release.” This is still a small card
movement, not an exploded workstation or spatial inspection. Do not fix the claim by
choosing a larger angle/percentage or counting five phase names. Rebuild the useful
composition, renegotiate fidelity, or report the result rejected.
